1. What you will learn

Leadership review turns a collection of findings into a decision.

Your task is not to make the proposal sound certain. It is to explain the problem, compare credible choices, show what the evidence supports and identify what must happen next.

A strong presentation allows a leader to answer:

What are we being asked to decide, why is that decision justified, and what responsibilities or conditions come with it?

In this chapter, you will learn to:

  • Present evidence separately from forecasts, assumptions and simulations.
  • Summarise value, cash costs and resource requirements accurately.
  • Explain the choice of solution and its alternatives.
  • Make dependencies and unresolved questions visible.
  • Answer leadership questions without overstating the case.
  • Record a bounded proceed, revise or defer decision.
  • Assemble your Business Automation Pilot Proposal.

Your required practice is to present the evidence, assumptions, costs and open questions, then record a proceed, revise or defer decision.

2. Lessons

Lesson 1 — Lead with the decision request, then explain the evidence

A leadership presentation should begin with the decision needed.

Compare:

“We have researched AI and prepared several documents.”

with:

“Revise the pilot-start proposal by confirming participants, review cover, source permission and support. Retain the template as the first comparator and keep the assistance arm gated.”

The second statement names the next decision and its boundary.

A useful presentation sequence is:

  1. Decision requested.
  2. Business problem and scope.
  3. Evidence and limitations.
  4. Options and recommendation.
  5. Value, costs and dependencies.
  6. Pilot measures and stop criteria.
  7. Owners and next decision point.

This is a reasoning structure, not a sales pitch.

Identify the status of each claim

Claim type Meaning Meridian example
Supplied scenario fact Explicit input within the fictional case Weekly cohort contains 120 completed enquiries
Calculation Result reproducible from supplied inputs Weekly effort is 38.4 staff hours
Forecast Estimate of future performance Assistance might release 16 process hours per four-week period
Target Result the pilot seeks Twelve-case effort at most 67.2 minutes
Simulation Supplied exercise outcome A practice packet contains a corrected unsupported promise
Unknown Not established Actual assistance review time or entitlement
Actual observation Evidence collected during real execution None supplied for Meridian’s proposed pilots

A scenario fact is not an observation from a real company. A calculation can be mathematically correct while its forecast inputs remain untested.

Worked example: correct an overstated opening

Overstatement:

“Our pilot proves that AI saves 13 hours a month.”

Correction:

“The planning model forecasts 13 net aggregate hours per four-week period after operating effort. The pilot has not run. Coverage, review, support and maintenance allowances require evidence.”

That correction preserves the useful number and its uncertainty.

If you use generative assistance to draft the presentation, check every figure and material claim against its source. Official guidance recommends grounding, uncertainty and verification, while noting that these measures do not eliminate unsupported output.1

Check L1: Why can a correct spreadsheet calculation still be an unsupported business claim?

Lesson 2 — Present costs and value without collapsing them into one number

Leadership needs to understand both the resource trade-off and the cash effect.

For Meridian, the main-case benefit is forecast capacity. Salaries remain fixed, and no removable expense has been identified.

The assistance option has:

  • More forecast time release than the template.
  • More implementation and operating requirements.
  • A positive capacity-equivalent balance under fictional rates.
  • An increase in cash expenditure.

Those statements can all be true.

Worked example: answer “Does it pay for itself?”

A suitable answer is:

“No cash-savings payback is established in the base case. The assistance option increases forecast cash expenditure by 1,960.55 CU over twelve four-week periods. Its positive resource-equivalent balance values staff time under the stated assumptions; it is not cash profit.”

Do not describe a capacity valuation as money available to fund a purchase.

Also explain the period. Twelve four-week periods contain 48 weeks, not a full calendar year.

Compare options fairly

Include:

  • A credible current or manual approach.
  • Process simplification.
  • The simplest plausible solution.
  • More capable alternatives where they address the task.
  • Implementation, review and operating costs.
  • Important non-financial consequences.

The Green Book’s appraisal guidance describes balanced consideration of options, uncertainty and impacts rather than choosing from a summary financial metric alone.2 Its public-sector requirements are not Meridian policies; the useful principle here is to explain trade-offs.

Check L2: Why might leadership prefer the template even if assistance releases more forecast hours?

Lesson 3 — Make dependencies into decisions and evidence requests

A dependency is a condition needed for a proposed next step.

A risk is an uncertain event or outcome that could affect objectives.

An unresolved question identifies what is not known. It becomes useful when you specify the evidence needed and the owner.

Compare:

“Data issues need attention.”

with:

“Operations must confirm the approved source owner, refresh arrangements and catalogue coverage before the assistance design relies on those sources.”

The second statement is actionable.

Worked example: a useful dependency table

Dependency Why it matters Evidence needed Owner
Source-use permission A readable file is not automatically approved for the proposed service Purpose-specific approval and effective access Source/access authority with IT
Review cover Every proposed offline output requires review Named primary and authorised backup Sales Lead
Assistance entitlement The feature and usage may require a specific plan Relevant edition, region, allowance and cost evidence IT and Finance
Support ownership Incidents and changes need a receiver Named operator, backup and escalation route IT Lead, proposed
Usable participant route Both users must perform required tasks Task-based checks of materials and controls Sales Lead with IT

Do not replace unknown costs with zero. Do not treat an action owner as evidence that the action has been completed.

Use sensitivity to guide questions

Chapter 5 showed that review time strongly affects value. Chapter 6 showed that changing the weight of failure containment can change the leading design.

Leadership should ask for evidence about those deciding factors.

A score of 73.33 does not prove readiness. A 45.00% readiness score and a 73.33-point selection index use different criteria and boundaries. They must not be compared as if they were the same measure.

Check L3: What must be added to “confirm support” to make it a useful next action?

Lesson 4 — Record a decision with scope, conditions and ownership

A leadership decision should include:

  • Decision category.
  • Scope and selected next step.
  • Rationale.
  • Conditions and dependencies.
  • Responsible owners.
  • Evidence needed for reconsideration.
  • Stop and restart responsibilities.
  • Status of the decision record.

Use the established categories:

Decision Meaning
Proceed Evidence supports a named next step
Revise Modify the proposal or complete evidence before advancing
Defer Do not advance while a critical dependency or stop-condition issue remains unresolved

A decision may differ by method. The template may be suitable for a larger offline comparison while the assistance method remains deferred.

Worked example: avoid ambiguous approval

Ambiguous:

“Proceed with AI.”

Useful:

“Proceed with technical assessment of the source-grounded internal-brief design. Confirm access, entitlement, review cover and support before any pilot run. Customer sending and live writes remain outside the scope.”

This does not grant broader authority.

A conditional proceed should not conceal a prerequisite that is still unresolved. State whether you are authorising preparation, testing or operation.

An example decision written from a synthetic case is not a report that a leadership meeting occurred. Likewise, a complete draft is not an independently reviewed proposal.

Check L4: Why should a decision name the evidence required for the next review?

2,304 ÷ 60 = 38.4 staff hours/week

For the four-week planning period:

120 × 4 = 480 completed cases

38.4 × 4 = 153.6 staff hours

Preparation is already included:

480 × 6 ÷ 60 = 48 preparation hours

480 × 12 ÷ 60 = 96 other initial hours

24 × 4 × 6 ÷ 60 = 9.6 additional rework hours

48 + 96 + 9.6 = 153.6 hours

The 24 reworked enquiries are partitioned into 14 missing-quantity/date cases, six copied-code errors and four outdated-stock checks. They are not an additional set to add again.

9. Solutions and explanations

Try the questions first. Open the solutions when you are ready to compare your reasoning.

Show solutions and explanations

Lesson checks

L1: Arithmetic can be correct while coverage, time reduction or cost inputs remain untested. The claim must preserve the input status and boundary.

L2: Its lower setup and operating requirements produce a stronger equivalent balance under the supplied assumptions. More forecast time release is not the only consideration.

L3: Name the support scope, operator, authorised backup, escalation route, completion evidence and point at which that evidence is needed.

L4: It makes reconsideration objective and gives owners a clear task. Without it, “revise” can become an indefinite instruction.

10. Chapter recap and next step

Leadership review is an evidence-based choice about what happens next.

A complete proposal can contain unknowns. Its strength lies in identifying them, assigning ownership and asking for a decision that fits the available evidence.
