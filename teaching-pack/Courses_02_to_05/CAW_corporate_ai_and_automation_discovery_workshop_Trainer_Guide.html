TRAINER GUIDE · DRAFT FOR TEAM REVIEW

Corporate AI and Automation Discovery Workshop

Produce a prioritized automation opportunity and a reviewed small pilot brief.

AudienceDepartment leaders and employee teams with one business process to improve
Entry requirementsSponsor identifies one process, attendees, available tools and approved synthetic examples before the workshop.
Course outcomeProduce a prioritized automation opportunity and a reviewed small pilot brief.
Class environmentFacilitator demonstration account, process worksheet and synthetic examples; attendees do not need production admin access.
Duration2 weeks · 12 hours total
Time split8 facilitated · 4 independent practice hours
Trainer note. Course examples and test values are synthetic teaching material. Use current official documentation and the actual training organization for product steps; distinguish mock results from observed results. Do not claim vendor certification, employment or client outcomes.

Capstone: Select one department process and define a small automation pilot.

ASSESSMENT AND PROJECT

What learners build

Baseline process, opportunity scorecard, sample assisted output and pilot brief with owner and acceptance measures.

Capstone scenarios to demonstrate

  • Missing input
  • incorrect generated answer
  • unsupported action
  • reviewer rejection.

Assessment weighting

  • Pilot Brief: 70%
  • Demonstration: 30%

The syllabus contains proposed completion rules. Confirm current grading, attendance, resubmission and credential wording with the academic lead before enrollment.

How to use the trainer guide

Each module contains the authored lesson plan followed by its trainer key. Use the linked module navigation, search, and print controls. Check the stated prerequisites and time budget; retain one portfolio of evidence through the capstone.

TEACH THE IDEA

A process baseline describes who performs each step, how long active work takes and where waiting occurs. Distinguish observed timing from estimates.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Synthetic follow-up tasks take 8, 10 and 12 active minutes: mean 10 minutes per task. At 100 tasks weekly, baseline active effort is 1000 minutes or 16.67 hours; this is a teaching example.

Teaching sequence

  • Baseline and recap: 10 minutes
  • Explain and predict: 15 minutes
  • Facilitator demonstration: 20 minutes
  • Team exercise: 40 minutes
  • Break: 10 minutes
  • Review and decision: 25 minutes

05

  1. Agree the process boundary with the sponsor.
  2. Draw trigger, roles, handoffs and completion.
  3. Time three sanitized examples.
  4. Record active and waiting time separately.
  5. Note variation and exceptions.

Expected result

A sponsor-reviewed process map and baseline sheet with units, sample size and observed or estimated labels.

Failure to test

A participant claims the task always takes ten minutes from one example. Record the evidence limitation.

Recovery to demonstrate

Repeat timing across a different case type and revise the baseline range.

TEACH THE IDEA

An assisted output is checked against its input before use. A well-written message can still invent an agreement, commitment or customer fact.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Synthetic input: customer asked for a demo next week; no date or discount agreed. A good draft proposes scheduling. A bad draft confirms Tuesday and a 20 percent discount.

Teaching sequence

  • Baseline and recap: 10 minutes
  • Explain and predict: 15 minutes
  • Facilitator demonstration: 20 minutes
  • Team exercise: 40 minutes
  • Break: 10 minutes
  • Review and decision: 25 minutes

Guided practical

01

Write a prompt using the approved facts.

02

Generate or use supplied contrasting drafts.

03

Highlight every factual assertion.

04

Check assertions against input.

05

Correct unsupported commitments.

  1. Write a prompt using the approved facts.
  2. Generate or use supplied contrasting drafts.
  3. Highlight every factual assertion.
  4. Check assertions against input.
  5. Correct unsupported commitments.
  6. Time generation plus review.

Expected result

Reviewed draft has no invented appointment or discount. Before-and-after timing includes correction work.

Failure to test

Insert a confidential customer note. Use a sanitized substitute unless the chosen tool and permissions are approved for it.

Recovery to demonstrate

Reject the bad draft and regenerate using explicit missing-date behavior.

Independent practice

Draft a support summary that distinguishes customer statements from confirmed findings.

TEACH THE IDEA

Prioritization combines value, readiness and failure consequence. A score guides discussion but cannot remove a missing permission or mandatory dependency.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Proposed formula: value plus readiness minus risk, each 1 to 5. Follow-up drafting 4+5-2=7; automatic refunds 5+2-5=2; support summaries 3+4-2=5. Drafting is the first discussion candidate.

Teaching sequence

  • Baseline and recap: 10 minutes
  • Explain and predict: 15 minutes
  • Facilitator demonstration: 20 minutes
  • Team exercise: 40 minutes
  • Break: 10 minutes
  • Review and decision: 25 minutes

Guided practical

01

List three opportunities.

02

Score each dimension and justify it.

03

Identify tool, data and access dependencies.

04

Apply any blocking conditions.

05

Draw the highest feasible pilot with exception and review paths.

  1. List three opportunities.
  2. Score each dimension and justify it.
  3. Identify tool, data and access dependencies.
  4. Apply any blocking conditions.
  5. Draw the highest feasible pilot with exception and review paths.

Expected result

A scorecard with rationale and a bounded pilot. The highest score is not an authorization to spend, publish or access client systems.

Failure to test

A high-scoring idea needs unavailable customer data. Mark it blocked until the dependency is resolved.

Recovery to demonstrate

Rescope to approved synthetic data and review whether the pilot still tests the intended value.

Independent practice

Change the risk weights and explain how the ranking changes.

TEACH THE IDEA

A pilot is a small experiment with an owner, sample, quality gate and next decision. Training attendance does not authorize a consulting implementation.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Proposed two-week pilot: 20 sanitized drafts, zero unsupported commitments after review, and a target of at least 20 percent lower median total task time. The target is a hypothesis, not promised savings.

Teaching sequence

  • Baseline and recap: 10 minutes
  • Explain and predict: 15 minutes
  • Facilitator demonstration: 20 minutes
  • Team exercise: 40 minutes
  • Break: 10 minutes
  • Review and decision: 25 minutes

Guided practical

01

State scope and exclusions.

02

Name sponsor, operator and reviewer.

03

Define timing and quality measurements.

04

Run four failure scenarios.

05

Schedule a two-week review.

  1. State scope and exclusions.
  2. Name sponsor, operator and reviewer.
  3. Define timing and quality measurements.
  4. Run four failure scenarios.
  5. Schedule a two-week review.
  6. Prepare a separately authorized discovery handoff.

Expected result

A pilot brief with measurable acceptance, stop conditions and review date. Record actual sample results before deciding to extend.

Failure to test

Reviewer rejects an output. The workflow returns for correction and does not send it.

Recovery to demonstrate

Suspend the pilot if critical exposure or unauthorized action occurs and let the sponsor decide the next step.

Independent practice

Define a different pilot for support summaries and choose a quality measure beyond speed.
