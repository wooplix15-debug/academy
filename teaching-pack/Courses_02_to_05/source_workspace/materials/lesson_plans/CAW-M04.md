# CAW-M04 Pilot and follow through
Version 0.2 | Team review required | Synthetic teaching case

## Purpose
- Define pilot acceptance and ownership
- Prepare a discovery handoff

## Prerequisite and capstone contribution
Use the previous module evidence as input: CAW-M03

Save this module evidence in the same course portfolio: Reviewed pilot brief.

## Explain the concept
A pilot is a small experiment with an owner, sample, quality gate and next decision. Training attendance does not authorize a consulting implementation.

## Demonstration and sample input
Proposed two-week pilot: 20 sanitized drafts, zero unsupported commitments after review, and a target of at least 20 percent lower median total task time. The target is a hypothesis, not promised savings.

## Live teaching sequence
- Baseline and recap: 10 minutes
- Explain and predict: 15 minutes
- Facilitator demonstration: 20 minutes
- Team exercise: 40 minutes
- Break: 10 minutes
- Review and decision: 25 minutes

## Guided lab
1. State scope and exclusions.
2. Name sponsor, operator and reviewer.
3. Define timing and quality measurements.
4. Run four failure scenarios.
5. Schedule a two-week review.
6. Prepare a separately authorized discovery handoff.

## Expected result
A pilot brief with measurable acceptance, stop conditions and review date. Record actual sample results before deciding to extend.

## Negative test
Reviewer rejects an output. The workflow returns for correction and does not send it.

## Recovery test
Suspend the pilot if critical exposure or unauthorized action occurs and let the sponsor decide the next step.

## Independent practice
Define a different pilot for support summaries and choose a quality measure beyond speed.

Budget: 60 minutes. Use 10 minutes planning, 35 practicing and 15 documenting.

## Submit and assess
Reviewed pilot brief.
- Scope is bounded
- Owner and review date exist
- Consulting discussion requires sponsor agreement

## Learner reflection
What separates training from consulting authorization?

Describe one failed input, your correction and evidence that the correction worked.
