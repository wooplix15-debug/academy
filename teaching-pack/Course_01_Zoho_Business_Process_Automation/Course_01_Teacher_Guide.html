CHAPTER 01 · Start with the work

Business Process Discovery

Teach learners to understand and measure a business process before choosing software or automation.

By the end, learners canOne-page current-state process map; eight unanswered discovery questions; fact versus assumption register; one baseline definition; named process owner.

Teach the idea

A process is a set of actions people take to produce an outcome for someone. Begin by naming that outcome, the event that starts the work, the people involved, the decisions they make, the information they need, and what counts as complete. Draw what happens today before drawing the future. A process map records work as it is actually done, including workarounds, queues, repeated entry, rework and exceptions. The formal procedure and the lived process can differ; discovery makes that difference discussable.

Separate an observation from an assumption. “Four people re-enter the same customer details” is an observation only if a learner can point to a record or interview that supports it. “This wastes 30 minutes per case” is an estimate until timing evidence supports it. A baseline states the measure, unit, source, sample and period: for example, median hours from enquiry receipt to first response, measured from a defined sample. A target needs a business owner and an agreed threshold. Do not promise savings before the team measures current work.

Worked example · Meridian Supply receives web enquiries. Sales staff copy details into …

Meridian Supply receives web enquiries. Sales staff copy details into a spreadsheet, check territory with a manager, contact the prospect, prepare a quote and pass won work to an implementation coordinator. Nobody has agreed what makes an enquiry qualified, how quickly it should be handled, or which system owns the customer record. Treat these as discovery questions, not facts to fill in for the client.

TRAINER WALK-THROUGH
  1. Ask the sponsor to describe the customer outcome and the event that starts the process.
  2. Interview the people doing the work. Ask them to walk through a recent ordinary case, then a case that went wrong.
  3. Draw actions, decisions, queues, systems, handoffs, rework and exceptions with a named owner at each step.
  4. Choose one baseline measure. Record its source, unit, sample period, owner and any missing evidence.

Guided practical

In pairs, role-play a 12-minute discovery interview: one learner is the Meridian sales manager, the other is the implementation consultant. Produce a current-state map, eight questions still requiring sponsor answers, a fact/assumption register and one proposed measure. Swap roles and compare maps.

SUBMISSION

One-page current-state process map; eight unanswered discovery questions; fact versus assumption register; one baseline definition; named process owner.

Review questions

  • Can the learner name the start event and successful outcome?
  • Does the map include at least one exception and one handoff?
  • Can another person reproduce the proposed measure from its definition?
Open trainer answer guidance

A defensible answer might measure median business hours from a valid enquiry arriving to the first documented human response. It must define valid enquiry, business hours, response evidence, sample period and data source. The target stays blank until the sponsor agrees it. Other measures can be acceptable when clearly defined and owned.

Watch for: Starting in CRM configuration; treating a manager's preferred screen as the process; turning guesses into time savings; drawing only the happy path.

CHAPTER 02 · Choose the right home for each job

Zoho Ecosystem and Solution Selection

Help learners distinguish the responsibilities of CRM, Books, Desk, People, Flow, Creator and Analytics, then choose a proportionate solution.

By the end, learners canApplication map; record ownership table; one architecture decision with alternatives considered; an explicit note about one feature or licence to verify.

Teach the idea

An application is a place to manage a class of work; it is not automatically the owner of every related record. CRM commonly supports customer-facing sales records. Books manages accounting records. Desk manages support tickets. People supports employee processes. Creator is used to build custom business applications. Flow connects supported events and actions across applications. Analytics brings data together for reporting. Zoho One is an administrative and licensing bundle; it does not remove the need to design ownership, access, process rules or integration behaviour.

Choose the simplest supported route that meets the real requirement. First ask whether an existing application feature can do it. Next assess a supported cross-application integration. Build custom logic only when a documented gap justifies its cost and maintenance. For every important record, name a system of record, an accountable owner, the permitted writers, and the route by which another application receives changes. Two copies can be useful; two uncoordinated owners create mismatches.

Worked example · For Meridian, the CRM may own the lead, account, contact and sales opp…

For Meridian, the CRM may own the lead, account, contact and sales opportunity. Books may own the accounting customer and invoice. Desk may own the support ticket. A closed-won deal may prompt a handoff, but the project team still needs to agree which fields cross the boundary, what happens when the handoff fails, and how staff know it needs attention.

TRAINER WALK-THROUGH
  1. Write the business task without naming a product.
  2. Identify the records created or changed and the team accountable for each one.
  3. Compare native configuration, supported integration and custom development against the task, access needs, failure handling and maintenance effort.
  4. Draw the system map. Mark record owner, connection direction, trigger, destination and person responsible for failed transfers.

Guided practical

Build a one-page application and ownership map for Meridian. Include CRM, Books, Desk and Analytics; add Creator or People only if you can state a concrete use. Mark one handoff and its failure owner. Explain why each app is present.

SUBMISSION

Application map; record ownership table; one architecture decision with alternatives considered; an explicit note about one feature or licence to verify.

Review questions

  • Does every important record have one accountable owner?
  • Can the learner explain why Flow or custom development is needed, if proposed?
  • Is a failed handoff assigned to a person or queue?
Open trainer answer guidance

There is no mandatory single diagram. A sound map shows CRM-owned sales information, Books-owned financial transactions, Desk-owned service tickets, and a defined handoff between them. Analytics consumes appropriately refreshed data and does not become the operational owner. Product capabilities and edition access must be checked in the actual organization.

Watch for: Assuming a bundle means every feature is available; storing every object in CRM; calling a one-way transfer a two-way sync; adding Creator before validating native options.

CHAPTER 03 · Set the operating context

Organisation and User Administration

Teach the foundational settings and user lifecycle choices that affect reliable day-to-day use.

By the end, learners canSettings register with decision owners; onboarding/offboarding checklist; named persona list; one date or locale check.

Teach the idea

An organization setup encodes how a business operates in a particular place. Locale, time zone, base currency, fiscal settings, business hours and holidays influence dates, reporting, sales targets and scheduled actions. Decide these with the business owner before importing data or configuring automation. Record decisions and test representative dates. A wrong time zone can make a scheduled task appear late; an inconsistent currency assumption can make reports misleading.

Treat user administration as a lifecycle: request, approve, provision, change role, suspend or remove access, and review. Each action needs an owner and a record. Give people individual accounts, the minimum access required for their job, and a named manager or administrator responsible for review. Avoid shared logins because they obscure who changed a record. A training organization should contain synthetic data and clearly identified trainer accounts.

Worked example · Meridian has representatives in two regions and a manager who approves…

Meridian has representatives in two regions and a manager who approves commercial exceptions. Ask whether business hours follow the company office, the prospect's location or another policy; which currency is used for reports; and who can invite or deactivate users. Keep the answers in an organization settings record rather than relying on memory.

TRAINER WALK-THROUGH
  1. Create a settings decision sheet for locale, time zone, currency, business hours and holidays.
  2. Sketch the user lifecycle and name the requester, approver, administrator and reviewer.
  3. Set up a training user for each learner persona using synthetic records.
  4. Test a scheduled date near a weekend or time-zone boundary and confirm what users see.

Guided practical

Create an organization checklist and a joiner/mover/leaver procedure for sales representative, sales manager and trainer. State what should happen to owned open records when someone leaves; leave the decision visible if the sponsor has not chosen a policy.

SUBMISSION

Settings register with decision owners; onboarding/offboarding checklist; named persona list; one date or locale check.

Review questions

  • Are regional/date choices tied to an accountable decision?
  • Is access associated with an individual rather than a team password?
  • Does the offboarding plan cover open records and connected access?
Open trainer answer guidance

A good submission lists undecided settings instead of guessing. Offboarding includes promptly disabling access, reviewing tokens/connections where relevant, reassignment of active records under an approved rule, and an auditable completion check.

Watch for: Using a real customer export for a class; creating an all-powerful learner account; confusing a user role with an organization setting.

CHAPTER 04 · Model records before adding fields

CRM Data Architecture

Build an understandable CRM structure that represents customer and sales work without needless duplication.

By the end, learners canEntity relationship diagram; field dictionary; one example query or user question the model can answer; decision log for conversion.

Teach the idea

A data model explains what records exist and how they relate. A lead is an unqualified or not-yet-converted enquiry under the chosen sales process; accounts represent organizations, contacts represent people, deals represent opportunities, and activities represent calls, tasks or meetings. Exact lifecycle choices should match business rules. Fields describe records; lookups connect records. A unique identifier helps match records safely. Every field should have a clear meaning, owner, format, allowed values, and reason for collection.

Design for the business question, not for a long field list. If a value describes the account, keep it with the account instead of copying it across every deal. If a value can vary per line in a quote, a single deal-level text field may not be enough. A lookup preserves a relationship; duplicating names creates drift. Layouts and required fields affect data entry, but a required field is only useful when users know the answer and the field is genuinely required at that step.

Worked example · Meridian needs to know the organization, the person to contact, the sa…

Meridian needs to know the organization, the person to contact, the sales opportunity, its products and the next activity. An account can relate to many contacts and deals. A deal can have activities. A product catalogue is shared reference data. Discuss whether a new web enquiry begins as a lead and when it becomes a contact/account/deal; avoid assuming every team uses the same conversion rule.

TRAINER WALK-THROUGH
  1. List the business entities and what each means in one sentence.
  2. Draw one-to-many relationships and mark lookup fields.
  3. Create a field dictionary with label, purpose, type, allowed values, owner, sensitivity and validation.
  4. Review the model against a real task: find all open deals for one account and see the last interaction.

Guided practical

Create a paper model for Account, Contact, Lead, Deal, Product and Activity. Add only fields needed to route, qualify, quote and hand off a request. Mark sensitive fields and fields whose allowed values require sponsor confirmation.

SUBMISSION

Entity relationship diagram; field dictionary; one example query or user question the model can answer; decision log for conversion.

Review questions

  • Can two records with the same display name be distinguished?
  • Are relationships represented as relationships rather than repeated free text?
  • Does each required field have a business reason and an entry point?
Open trainer answer guidance

Several models can work. The learner should describe entity meaning, identifiers, relationships, validation and lifecycle. Do not grade on copying a memorized sample data model if it fails the business use case.

Watch for: Making a custom field for every question; using email as a guaranteed permanent identifier; treating a required field as a data quality strategy.

CHAPTER 05 · Test access as a user

Roles, Profiles and Record Access

Separate what a user can do from which records the user can see, then verify both with representative accounts.

By the end, learners canApproved access matrix; test cases; observed evidence for at least one allowed and one denied action per persona; list of unresolved policy decisions.

Teach the idea

Access is layered. A profile generally controls permissions to perform actions and access features; a role helps define organizational hierarchy and visibility; organization-wide defaults and sharing rules determine record reach; field-level settings can restrict specific information. CHAPTER 17 · Define the number before drawing the chart

Reporting and Zoho Analytics

Build reports with clear grain, metric definitions, filters, joins, refresh expectations and access.

By the end, learners canMetric dictionary; query/report definition; reconciliation samples; dashboard screenshot or sketch; access check.

Teach the idea

A report is only as meaningful as its data grain. One row might represent one lead, one deal, one activity, or one line item. Joining a deal to several activities can repeat deal value; summing the repeated amount inflates totals. Define what a row represents, how related tables join, whether unmatched records appear, and whether a metric is a count, sum, ratio or duration. Write the formula in plain language before building the chart.

A dashboard adds presentation and refresh choices. State date range, timezone, filters, owner scope, refresh time, data source and who can view it. Reconcile one report result against source records. Ratios require a defined numerator and denominator; a conversion rate can change based on cohort, dates and what counts as converted. A chart is not a decision until someone knows what action to take and what limitations apply.

Worked example · Meridian asks how many enquiries became qualified and how many deals a…

Meridian asks how many enquiries became qualified and how many deals are awaiting quote approval. Learners define the cohort and stage evidence, then check a sample manually. If joining deals to multiple activities, they demonstrate how repeated rows affect deal amount and choose a correct grain or aggregation.

TRAINER WALK-THROUGH
  1. Write the decision question and the metric formula in words.
  2. State source table, row grain, relationship/join, filters and refresh schedule.
  3. Build a small report and reconcile selected rows or totals to CRM.
  4. Share with a test persona; explain limitations and action owner.

Guided practical

Create one lead conversion report and one activity/deal report in a training workspace or in a spreadsheet mock. Include date scope, denominator, filters, source, refresh time and a join-duplication check.

CHAPTER 18 · Prove it can be operated

Testing, Rollout and Administration

Prepare user acceptance, permissions, release readiness, training, support ownership and continuous improvement.

By the end, learners canRequirements-to-test trace; UAT results; defect log; release and recovery checklist; role-based user guide; support owner; improvement backlog.

Teach the idea

Testing asks whether the delivered configuration meets a defined requirement. A test case states precondition, persona, action, input, expected result and evidence. Include ordinary, invalid, duplicate, denied, exception and recovery cases. User acceptance is a business decision made by authorized users; a trainer's successful demo alone is insufficient. Defects need severity, owner, reproduction steps, workaround and retest result. Keep training separate from changing a live system without permission.

A release plan names environment, scope, dependencies, approver, backup or recovery option, communications, smoke tests and go/no-go conditions. Product deployment features differ; a sandbox does not guarantee every data record or feature is deployable. Verify what moves, what must be recreated and how rollback works before release. The support handover should identify an administrator, user help route, integration owner, monitoring, known limitations and scheduled review. Improvement uses feedback and measured outcomes, not feature accumulation.

Worked example · Meridian's final project must handle a valid enquiry, duplicate, missi…

Meridian's final project must handle a valid enquiry, duplicate, missing region, denied record access, approved quote, failed integration, Desk escalation and a dashboard total that reconciles. Before a simulated launch, learners review critical access and data-quality failures. If one fails, the release decision is “hold or fix,” not “launch because the demo looked good.”

  4. Prepare user guidance, rollout communication, support rota and recovery plan.
