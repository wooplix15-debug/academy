## Writing standard

Write for a capable learner who is new to this exact topic.

- Use simple professional English.
- One idea per paragraph. Aim for 40–100 words per paragraph.
- Define a technical term the first time it appears, then use it consistently.
- Put the reason before the rule when that helps understanding.
- Replace policy-style sentences with direct learner instructions.
- Replace long inline examples with a short table, code block or labelled example.
- Use headings that describe the learner’s question, such as “How a citation supports an answer”.
- Use a worked example in this order: situation, supplied facts, reasoning, result, common mistake.
- Use exercises in this order: goal, inputs, steps, expected result, check your work.
- Keep warnings short and specific. Do not repeat the same warning in several sections.
- Preserve important boundaries: permissions, approval, tenant separation, source freshness, missing evidence, retries and audit records.

## Required chapter shape

1. What you will learn
2. Lessons, with clear subheadings
3. Visual explanation, with a plain-text explanation
4. Worked case
5. Guided practice
6. Independent challenge
7. Common problems and recovery
8. Check your understanding
9. Solutions and explanations
10. Recap and next step
11. Glossary and further reading

## Do not do these things

- Do not shorten by deleting required concepts or safety controls.
- Do not turn explanations into a long list of bullets.
- Do not invent evidence for a claim because it sounds likely.
- Do not write trainer instructions, sales copy or generic AI disclaimers.
- Do not claim that a live product, model or API was used unless the source explicitly records an observed result.
